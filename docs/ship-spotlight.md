# Ship Spotlight

Implemented 5 November 2026. **Free data sources only**: existing AIS, CC0 Wikidata and CC BY 4.0 Digitraffic Portnet. No paid subscription, credits, trial or per-installation data key.

## Use it

Turn on **Spotlight a ship** in the Harbor Map settings. The same harbor shares one subject across installations and framing choices. Labels can remain off: the featured name is always prioritized. Turn the switch off for the ordinary harbor overview. Spotlight never moves the map: it keeps each harbor's reviewed view and zoom, picks a subject that is visible in it (safely visible ships compete together, never under the harbor card) and adds the large annotation. Context ships keep their normal size and theme styling. A layout too small to show the subject and its card shows the plain overview instead. No suitable fresh candidate means an explicit overview fallback, never a frozen or invented ship.

The **Port of Call harbor card stays at bottom left**, showing its name, country and the count of distinct vessels recently seen near it. Spotlight is a separate native inverse annotation: ship name, unlabeled type, flag/registry country, voyage and ETA, current speed, optional sourced highlights, then ship particulars. Countries accompany port names when known; missing origin/country components are omitted. Service-entry dates are not presented as build dates. Compact views show fewer supporting facts.

Fintraffic/source and license links sit on the map beside the complete OpenStreetMap credit. Wikidata/CC0 credit is in the About/settings description. The card stays four logical pixels from the map bottom-left edge; credits wrap beside it. Report/fetch timestamps remain in the API rather than on either card. A mandatory stronger connector links the annotation to its geographically exact featured hull. Context ships remain grey (hollow at one bit) and may be covered by the annotation; the selected ship and source credits remain clear.

The [ranked fact proposal](ship-spotlight-content.md) explains which facts are most interesting, their free-source limitations and the provisional shortlist for user review. The API still retains the richer facts and reference enrichment; the overlay deliberately shows a small subset.

## What the facts mean

- Positions, speed, heading/course and navigation status retain the dynamic report time. Focus requires a position younger than 15 minutes and a feed younger than the configured freshness threshold.
- IMO numbers require the seven-digit checksum. Unknown/saturated dimensions and draught, malformed ETAs and unavailable text are omitted. Length/beam derive from antenna distances. Digitraffic draught is decimetres converted to metres; AISStream's decoded floating-point draught is metres, not divided again. AIS ETA has no year. A separate bounded heuristic infers a nearby year for display, as detailed below; this is still a reported intention rather than a schedule.
- Destination, ETA and draught expire independently after six hours. Identity/dimensions expire after seven days. Each field retains its source and report timestamp. A new position/name cannot refresh a destination; out-of-order static messages cannot overwrite newer facts. An explicit unknown clears a previous value. A crew can keep broadcasting an obsolete destination or ETA: even a recent report is not independent voyage confirmation.
- Previous port is absent from standard AIS. The optional Finnish Portnet join requires an exact IMO or MMSI, the selected Finnish port, a recent record with an actual arrival before the observation, and no actual departure before it. An IMO mismatch rejects the record even if MMSI matches. Planned calls and old calls elsewhere do not supply origin. This is a **reported previous port**, not a reconstructed voyage. Otherwise origin remains unknown in the API and the overlay shows only the reported destination.
- Wikidata ship lookup requires a unique exact IMO statement. It can add class, builder, operator and service-entry year. Deprecated and ended-role statements are excluded; reference information is not a verified current commercial registry. It can supply a registry-country reference and shipyard-country facts, with explicit provenance; neither is an independently verified current flag or build year. Live port lookup requires a unique exact UN/LOCODE and a usable English name. Earth coordinates are optional; absent, invalid or conflicting coordinates are omitted. Ambiguous destinations remain raw reported text. A bundled [CC0 reference](port-reference.md) supplies 40,926 unambiguous coded-location names and 247 country labels without HTTP requests, alongside reviewed exact aliases. Airport records and conflicting matches are excluded. There are no fuzzy ship-name joins, guessed tracks, inferred origins or photo-license assumptions.

The JSON card retains identifiers, sources and timestamps even when the compact display omits some facts. Destination coordinates are optional reference metadata; no straight line is presented as a sailing route. No personal/agent/customs fields from Portnet are retained.

## Selection

The fixed `harbor` view does **not** move the camera for Spotlight. All safely visible eligible ships compete together; there is no central rectangle, centrality bonus or distance-to-center ranking. The visibility mask still excludes 6% side margins, 8% top/bottom margins and an approximate bottom-left card area. Smaller layouts retain their actual visibility/annotation-fit checks and can fall back to overview.

Eligibility requires a position less than 15 minutes old, a usable name and at least two facts/type entries. Normally the subject stays for up to one hour. A held subject can be replaced early when an available fresh alternative scores at least 15 joint-priority points higher, whether or not the held ship is moving. Only those actual upgrades enter an early-replacement draw; small fluctuations retain the held subject. This comparison respects the normal repeat-avoidance pool; it does not rotate through recently featured ships just to force motion.

**Available information: up to 60 final points.** The information score (0–100, multiplied by 0.6) reuses the actual card presentation and cached enrichment, without network requests. It rewards meaningful type and flag (5 each), a readable destination/activity/previous port (15; an unresolved code or opaque all-caps token gets only 5), displayable ETA (10), length and width (10 each), up to two displayed reference highlights (15 each), and sourced build/shipyard country (15). Missing or expired fields, hidden builder-only records, identifiers and pending lookups add nothing. This scores content available to the full card; compact layouts may omit some details.

**Movement: up to 30 points.** Missing speeds or speeds below 1 knot earn no motion points; speeds from 1 to under 3 knots earn 15; 3 knots and above earn 30. This avoids treating 0.5-knot drift or slow dock manoeuvring as strong evidence of travel. The bonus caps at 3 knots, so higher speed is not intrinsically more interesting. It remains evidence from reported AIS speed, not a verified departure, track or voyage event.

Information and movement form a joint priority (0–90). First try ships not selected within 24 hours, relaxing only if none exist. Within that pool, ships more than 15 joint-priority points below the best do not enter the final draw. This lets a useful mover compete with a somewhat better-described stationary ship, while a sparse mover does not automatically beat a richly described stationary subject.

**Variety and size: up to 10 points.** Time since feature adds up to 4, variety relative to the last three ship types adds up to 4, and local length percentile adds up to 2. The top five final scores enter a deterministic draw with squared-score weights, seeded by harbor, hourly UTC bucket and MMSI. Selection/history persist across restarts; history is bounded to 64 entries per harbor.

Proactive enrichment plans a rolling shortlist of up to three safely visible, uncached IMO-identified candidates per port per hour, prioritizing movement and useful card content. Completed positive/negative matches leave the next shortlist, so other candidates get a turn. Plans persist across collector restarts; unfinished work is reconstructed, and empty lists consume no allowance. The same planner runs in both server variants. A queued lookup earns no speculative selection bonus.

The chosen ship is protected from marker/payload truncation. The annotation planner finds room around its actual projected position without moving the fixed harbor camera. Legacy non-`harbor` API scales retain their older framing for compatibility and are absent from the current recipe settings.

## Free enrichment operations

One asynchronous worker shares the collector and storage. HTTP snapshot generation uses cached data only. Screen-requested facts and unresolved destinations use the primary 128-job queue; proactive candidates use a separate 32-job queue. Actual screen work runs first and can promote an already queued candidate without duplicate lookups. Wikidata requests are spaced by at least one second, with a persisted hard cap of 500 requests/day per collector, independent of installations. Speculative work leaves at least 250 requests for actual cards and destination names, checked both before admission and before every HTTP request. Budget exhaustion is not cached as a missing record. Positive results cache 30 days, absent/ambiguous matches seven days and transient failures one hour. Old coordinate-required port misses are reconsidered through a versioned negative-cache key without resetting ship records. There is no account or charge fallback. All structured facts link to their entity and CC0; a cached hit preserves its original fetch date.

Portnet is polled at most once/hour only for the Finnish provider, with failure backoff. Only the relevant Finnish calls' identifiers, port codes, reported nationality code and actual event times are stored, for at most seven days. Source credit and CC BY 4.0 remain visible. Optional-source failures cannot stop AIS collection or block a map response. Run one ingestion/enrichment leader as documented; multiple independent replicas are not the supported deployment.

## Country labels and ETA inference · 5 November follow-up

The annotation uses native TRMNL inverse paint: a highlighted inverse surface that adapts to the screen theme, including dark mode. Every fact has a category label; speed uses “knots,” and “Length × width” explains beam without requiring nautical terminology. Port labels include countries when a reviewed reference or unique exact-code lookup supports them. Hanko, Finland → Tilbury, United Kingdom is the archived PLYCA example. A reviewed exact CC0 reference for PLGDN resolves Gdańsk, Poland without inventing destination coordinates. Unknown or ambiguous destinations remain reported text.

`focus.eta` preserves the original yearless AIS value. Additive `focus.eta_estimate` resolves candidates in the report year and its adjacent years, accepting only dates between two days before and 90 days after the actual static report. Reports must still satisfy the six-hour voyage TTL. The nearest candidate is chosen, December/January and leap dates are validated, and an inferred time older than two days relative to snapshot time is omitted. The API marks `year_inferred: true` and records the inference rule and inferred UTC instant. The display uses month/day/time with “Reported ETA.” An August ETA rebroadcast in November is omitted rather than rolled into the next August. These windows are a documented heuristic, not a claim about confirmed arrivals, delays or all voyage lengths.

Flag/country enrichment remains entirely free and asynchronous. Regular ship-station MMSIs only are eligible for a MID lookup through Wikidata property P2979. SAR devices, associated craft, aids, group identities and unknown formats are excluded. The display labels the result “Flag (AIS)” and the API marks it inferred. A radio area without its own ISO country code, such as Madeira, resolves through its country reference to Portugal; the allocation area is retained separately. Independent territory registries with ISO codes remain distinct. No radio allocation is presented as independently verified current registration.

A relevant actual Finnish Portnet call can provide a reported nationality code, displayed as “Flag.” Wikidata P8047 supplies an optional registry-country reference; ended, future, deprecated and ambiguous statements are omitted. A registry reference is retained separately if an AIS-derived country is displayed, and supplies “Registry (Wikidata)” when no suitable radio-country inference is available.

Construction enrichment uses an explicit P1071 creation site or a P176 builder explicitly classified as a shipyard (Q190928), then its unique country reference. Corporate headquarters are never substituted for a yard. Direct-site results are labeled “Built in”; builder-yard fallbacks are labeled “Shipyard country,” preserving the distinction from a historical build-country claim. Historical border changes are not reconstructed. PLYCA's exact-IMO reference identifies Szczecin Shipyard and Poland; a company-only builder record does not automatically establish a build country.

Country-aware enrichment uses `v2:` cache keys so previous 30-day records without these fields refresh automatically. Existing database tables and position data need no reset. The one-request-per-second, 500-request/day hard cap and graceful optional-source failure behavior remain in effect. No additional credentials or paid dependencies are needed.

The original AIS archive coordinates, report times and snapshot times are unchanged. Country reference metadata was fetched later on 5 November and has its own `reference_enriched_at`/source fetch dates. Refresh it with `scripts/enrich_archives.py`; this helper never replays or re-dates positions. Compact camera fallback now scores visible context cells as well as distance, so a separate annotation does not unnecessarily move all other vessels out of the frame.

## Count and copy contract

`focus.presentation` provides the ordered human-readable copy without changing source facts, timestamps or inference provenance. It preserves zero speed, removes unavailable route components, separates service-entry highlights from construction years and supports independent length/width facts. The label “vessels seen near this port” refers to distinct unexpired MMSIs in the selected observation window within the reviewed harbor rectangle, not every vessel physically present or every glyph visible after decluttering. Changing map scale or the shared subject can change that selected area. Empty/stale/unavailable diagnostics remain visible when applicable.

The compact map source footer wraps rather than truncating the OpenStreetMap or data-license credits. The preview page's historical/state-test banner is outside `.screen`; screenshots show the product layout, with provenance retained in the fixture/documentation and presented in their surrounding captions. No fixture is replayed as current public data.

All Spotlight facts use the same native small-label size, with bold field labels and a larger ship name. Display copy omits repeated “reported” qualifiers; the API preserves source/report timestamps and inferred-country/ETA provenance. Draught is excluded from the Spotlight card and `focus.presentation`, even when the upstream static data includes it.

The one-hour hold is a maximum on identity, not position: every snapshot rechecks fresh positions and safe visibility. A ship leaving the view is replaced on the next snapshot without waiting for the hour. The 15-minute device refresh can still show an earlier position until the next screen update.

Overview markers may remain visible for up to 60 minutes, while Spotlight requires a position younger than 15 minutes. A fresh screen rendering does not refresh the underlying AIS report. For example, a 36.6-knot report from 25 minutes ago can remain on the map but cannot become the highlighted subject.

## Reviewed route and activity terms

[Destination-term review](destination-terms.md) covers sampled labels in all sixteen harbors. Exact reviewed route strings receive route captions, activities receive activity captions, and inferred local ferry shorthand is explicitly marked “Likely route”. Source URLs, inference flags and raw destination values remain in the API; route captions never create actual previous-port records or infer a current sailing direction. Both ordinary labels and Spotlight share the same dictionary.


## Glanceability improvements · 11 November 2026

The port card now reads **N vessels in area · M on map** when decluttering, framing or overlays hide some markers. M is calculated after map projection and Spotlight placement; it counts visible hulls, not the number of labels or the API's forty-marker cap. The fixed harbor camera and heading behavior are preserved, including the existing decorative resting angle when bearing is unavailable.

A Spotlight ship can show a short observed trail. Both storage adapters retain at most twelve real fixes per vessel over thirty minutes, sampled no more often than every two minutes after at least 0.025 nautical miles of movement. Speeds below one knot do not add samples. Gaps over six minutes and jumps beyond a conservative 65-knot envelope reset the trail. No position is interpolated, extrapolated, or presented as an actual previous port. At most 600 recent vessel tracks survive maintenance/checkpointing; history yields to current data under the existing eight-megabyte checkpoint budget. Only the selected ship's small trail enters a display response. Cold starts or insufficient observations simply omit it.

Reference matching now also supports exact MMSI (Wikidata P587) when IMO is missing. No name matching is introduced. Additional CC0 facts include maximum passenger/person capacity (P1083, unambiguous unitless quantity), explicitly ended former names (P1448/P2561), explicitly ended former uses (P366), and participation in notable events (P1344). These are ranked ahead of generic operator/class facts; at most two highlights are displayed. Service-entry age is approximate and anchored to the observation year, never mislabeled as construction age. Existing ship records refresh gradually under the unchanged 500-request/day budget; countries and ports keep their caches. New facts are optional and do not block an HTTP response.

Long ordinary voyage labels can occupy two lines. Complete wording is preferred, then reviewed prefixes may be shortened, then a verified origin→destination pair may show only **To destination**. A bidirectional route is never converted into a claimed next stop. No route row relies on hover text or a trailing ellipsis.

Port facts are stable for at least an hour. For selections whose full harbor cycle takes longer, their period follows that cycle, so every harbor can reach every fact. Rotation is stateless and cacheable, including Finnish responses forwarded through the world backend; private installation identifiers are unnecessary.

Built-in harbor landmarks use the [reviewed CC0 catalog](landmark-sources.json) and place at most two labels, using verified Wikidata coordinates. There is no landmark setting. Islands and water bodies use their source's representative point, not a navigation waypoint. Landmarks never move the camera and yield to vessel markers, labels, information cards and credits; the smallest layouts omit them. Wikidata/CC0 attribution remains in About/settings.

A bounded review queue records unresolved destination strings from candidate map markers, grouped by harbor. It keeps thirty-two terms per harbor for seven days, with up to sixty-four distinct vessel identifiers retained internally per term and half-hour deduplication of request frequency. **GET /api/v1/destination-review** exposes only terms and aggregate counts, not identifiers or tracks. Run `python scripts/review_destinations.py` to rank both deployed profiles without an AIS credential. The collector is not self-learning: reviewed additions remain explicit in the dictionary. Local Shift/Local Shifts now read **Activity: Harbor movements**; other reviewed working notes are normalized too.
